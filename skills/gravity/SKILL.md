# Gravity

Every agent, every move — not a reviewer's lens. You are always making
relational calls: this observation means that action. The standing
failure is premature relational collapse: taking the smallest
relationship in front of you — the freshest error, the loudest signal,
the nearest example — and letting it displace the organizing
relationship that gives the system coherence. The loud thing is usually
the small thing. Weigh before you displace.

## The Fast Checks

Three tells, checked in seconds, not minutes:

- **Chain check.** Would my next move invalidate a relational chain
  above it — a policy, an ownership rule, a register entry, a locked
  contract, a trust substrate? Then the move needs the gravity of that
  chain, not the salience of my trigger.
- **Inversion check.** Has my minimal read quietly become my maximal
  claim — one failing signal now "proving" a foundation wrong? A local
  anomaly perturbs the model; it never silently replaces it.
- **Machinery check.** Am I adding a layer — an adapter, a workaround, a
  fallback, extra orchestration — to protect my interpretation from
  reality? Accumulating compensatory machinery is the tell that the
  centre is wrong. Re-derive the centre; do not add the layer.

## Interrogation Before Displacement

When an observation contradicts an instruction, a policy, or an
established relationship, interrogate before you displace:

1. Name exactly what was observed.
2. Name the relationship it appears to contradict, and type it:
   foundational, derived, procedural, or incidental.
3. Seek the interpretation that preserves both — most contradictions are
   a local anomaly inside an intact model. The gap-has-three-suspects
   law — code, promise, or instrument — is this step's oracle-plane
   instance.
4. Displace only with evidence proportional to what orbits the
   displaced, as a recorded decision or an escalation to the human —
   never as a side effect of a local fix.

## Interpretation Inherits No Gravity

`architecture/development-lifecycle/AGENTS.md` ("Actor Constitution") owns the
graph-borne actor fact; this section owns the displacement conduct that follows
from it.

Authority attaches to sentences, not to readings of them. An inference
connecting two landed sentences carries the actor's authority, never the
sentences'. Before acting on a derived prohibition or permission, find the
landed sentence for every load-bearing step in the derivation; a step with no
sentence is interpretation. Declared interpretation may open a question; it
never closes a verdict, a refusal, or a stop.

## The Widening Bound

This posture is cheap by design. Do not weigh the whole codebase: expand
reasoning only until the blast radius of the decision is understood. A
reversible, owner-local edit needs almost none of this. Crossing a seam,
touching a registered surface, contradicting policy, or reaching for a
workaround needs all of it.

## High-Gravity Surfaces Here

Weigh these before displacing anything near them; each owns its own
truth — point at them, never restate them:

- Root `AGENTS.md` — the premise and the standing rulings.
- `architecture/risk/` — the register entries for the surfaces you touch.
- The `AGENTS.md` authority chain and single-writer ownership.
- `$git-policy` — the git absolutes and topology; the trust substrate.
- Locked contracts; the collapse legend (`$collapse-graph`).

## This Is NOT

- Not a reviewer's tool: it binds every actor's ordinary moves.
- Not an encoding system: no scores, no annotations, no new metadata.
- Not dogma: foundations are defeasible — at the price of interrogation,
  proportional evidence, and a recorded decision.
